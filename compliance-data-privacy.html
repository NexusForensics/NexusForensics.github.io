Compliance & Data Privacy

Local-first evidence software designed to reduce data exposure.

Nexus Social Recon is built around a local-first deployment model and investigator-directed capture. It preserves what the investigator sees and selects, and it is upfront about how: screen capture first, with page structure and the original media files used to make the record complete and verifiable.

Privacy Architecture

A closed-loop model for sensitive investigative material.

Nexus Forensics Inc. does not collect, process, host, or access the evidence analyzed by the software. The platform is designed so case data remains on the user's workstation or on infrastructure controlled by the client organization, and so captures are based on visible, user-selected content.

Local evidence handling

Evidence files, local indexes, metadata, reports, and review material are handled on the investigator's computer or within a client-controlled private network, not in a vendor cloud. The few services the software contacts are listed below.

No vendor access

Nexus Forensics Inc. has no custody of investigative or consumer data analyzed by the software, reducing third-party data exposure and vendor access risk.

Encrypted local repositories

Case vaults and the local index are encrypted at rest with AES-256. Available on macOS today; the Windows version is in development.

Retention stays with the agency

The agency decides how long case files are kept and when they are deleted. Case files stay on the agency's own computers, and deleting a capture is recorded in the case audit log.

Investigator-visible capture

The software preserves what the investigator sees and selects: on-screen content, the files the page serves for it, or a selected local file. It is not designed to defeat logins, privacy settings, or other access controls.

Outside Connections

What can leave the investigator's computer.

Evidence is processed locally. The software contacts the services below, only for the purposes shown. None of them receives case files; the only evidence-derived text that can leave is a place phrase the investigator chooses to look up.

How Capture Works

Capture what the investigator sees, and be upfront about how.

Nexus Social Recon captures the content available to the investigator in the app's own browser. The investigator navigates, selects and starts every capture, and Nexus states the methods it used in each report. Nexus Forensics is taking steps to comply with platform terms of service, and the investigator remains responsible for following them.

Screen first

Screenshots and recordings of the page as the investigator sees it are the core of every capture.

Page structure for accuracy

The page's structure (the DOM) is read to find where a post, comment or profile section starts and ends, to read labels, and to crop cleanly. It helps organize what is on screen.

Original media as served

Where the platform serves the original photo or video for what is on screen, Nexus downloads that exact file instead of settling for a screen grab. For YouTube it uses yt-dlp to download the exact video being served. Every file is hashed and tied to its source URL.

Investigator-directed

The investigator chooses the platform, the account and the content. Multi-item captures such as scrolling capture run only when the investigator starts them.

Methods stated in the report

Each report includes a methodology section describing how the evidence was captured, so a reviewer can see what was screen-captured, what was read from the page, and what was downloaded.

Line-hashed audit log

Every investigator action is written to the case's audit log, and each entry is chained to the one before it with SHA-256. An edited, removed, or added entry breaks the chain, and the Case Activity Report shows where.

Canadian Alignment

Built to support PIPEDA privacy principles.

The local-first architecture is designed around data minimization, limiting collection, limiting use, limiting disclosure, limiting retention, and safeguards proportionate to sensitive evidence material.

PIPEDA means the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act, Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. Learn more from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

PIPEDA Requirement Nexus Forensics Compliance Step
Principle 4: Limiting Collection Evidence data is recorded from visible, user-selected content and processed locally or on client-controlled infrastructure, reducing unnecessary vendor collection of investigative or personal information.
Principle 5: Limiting Use, Disclosure, and Retention Nexus Forensics does not transmit evidence to vendor cloud systems for processing. The agency decides how long case files are kept and when they are deleted.
Principle 7: Safeguards Case vaults and the local index are encrypted at rest using AES-256 encryption on the investigator's workstation (macOS today; Windows in development).
Accountability support Audit logs with line hashing (each entry is chained to the one before it with SHA-256, so an edited, removed, or added entry breaks the chain), local case records, file hashes, and controlled deployment boundaries help agencies document how evidence was stored, reviewed, retained, and protected.

United States Alignment

A privacy posture shaped for CCPA, CPRA, and state privacy laws.

OCR, face and object matching, indexing, and reports run on the investigator's own computer, using models bundled with the app. The software does not send analyzed evidence to Nexus Forensics. With that, the product is structured to avoid selling or sharing personal information for advertising or third-party data purposes.

CCPA means the California Consumer Privacy Act, which gives California residents rights over personal information collected by covered businesses. CPRA means the California Privacy Rights Act, which amended and expanded California's privacy framework. Learn more from the California Attorney General and the California Privacy Protection Agency.

CCPA / CPRA Requirement Nexus Forensics Compliance Step
Consumer data control and access rights Nexus Forensics does not host or control customer evidence data. Agencies retain custody of case data on their workstation or private infrastructure and manage access through their own policies.
No selling or sharing of personal information The software does not transmit analyzed evidence to Nexus Forensics for advertising, resale, cross-context behavioral advertising, or third-party data sharing.
Data minimization and purpose limitation Processing is limited to visible, user-selected evidence inside the investigative workflow controlled by the client organization, with no vendor cloud processing or unrelated secondary use by Nexus Forensics.
Sensitive personal information protection Case vaults, media, metadata, and the local index are designed for encrypted storage at rest (macOS today; Windows in development).
Automated analysis and profiling risk controls Face, image, and cross-case matching operate on locally preserved case material on the investigator's own computer, rather than a public or cloud database.
